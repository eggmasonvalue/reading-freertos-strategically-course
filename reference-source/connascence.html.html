Two components are <strong>connascent</strong> if changing one forces a change in the other to keep the system correct.<sup class="cite"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Ranked below by <b>strength</b> (how hard to refactor). The strategic move is almost always: <em>convert a stronger form into a weaker one.</em></p>2627  <p class="band">Static connascence — visible in the source (weakest → strongest)</p>28  <table class="cx">29    <thead><tr><th>Form</th><th>Two parties must agree on…</th><th>Weaken it by…</th><th>FreeRTOS specimen</th></tr></thead>30    <tbody>31      <tr><td class="k">Name</td><td>the <em>name</em> of a thing. Rename the entity, update every reference. Unavoidable; the weakest.</td><td>— (this is the floor; aim to land here)</td><td>every call to <code class="sp">xQueueSend</code></td></tr>32      <tr><td class="k">Type</td><td>the <em>type</em> of a thing (param, return, field).</td><td>strong static typing makes it compiler-checked — cheap</td><td><code class="sp">QueueHandle_t</code> in every signature</td></tr>33      <tr><td class="k">Meaning</td><td>the <em>meaning</em> of a value — a magic number/sentinel both sides interpret.</td><td>name the constant → becomes connascence of <b>Name</b></td><td>raw <code>0</code>/<code>1</code> upgraded to <code class="sp">pdPASS</code>/<code class="sp">queueSEND_TO_BACK</code></td></tr>34      <tr><td class="k">Position</td><td>the <em>order</em> of values — positional args, tuple/array slots.</td><td>pass a named struct → becomes connascence of <b>Name</b></td><td>the 4 ordered args of <code class="sp">xQueueGenericSend</code></td></tr>35      <tr><td class="k">Algorithm</td><td>a shared <em>algorithm/protocol</em> — both sides compute or sequence the same way.</td><td>share one implementation; encapsulate the protocol</td><td>the <code class="sp">pxHigherPriorityTaskWoken</code> ISR yield protocol; a checksum both ends run</td></tr>36    </tbody>37  </table>3839  <p class="band">Dynamic connascence — only visible at run time (stronger; hunt these first)</p>40  <table class="cx">41    <thead><tr><th>Form</th><th>Two parties must agree on…</th></tr></thead>42    <tbody>43      <tr><td class="k">Execution (order)</td><td>the <em>order</em> in which things run — call <code>init()</code> before <code>use()</code>.</td></tr>44      <tr><td class="k">Timing</td><td>the <em>timing</em> of execution — a race, a required delay, a deadline.</td></tr>45      <tr><td class="k">Value</td><td>several <em>values</em> must change together to stay consistent (an invariant across variables).</td></tr>46      <tr><td class="k">Identity</td><td>multiple entities must refer to the <em>same instance</em> (same object, not just equal).</td></tr>47    </tbody>48  </table>49  <p style="font-size:0.9rem;color:var(--ink-soft)">Dynamic forms are generally stronger than static ones: the compiler can't see them, so they fail at run time and resist refactoring tools. When you spot one, that's your first refactor target.</p>5051  <p class="band">The three properties (how you reason about any instance)</p>52  <table class="cx">53    <tbody>54      <tr><td class="k">Strength</td><td>How hard is it to refactor away? Weaker (Name) is easier to change than stronger (Algorithm, or any dynamic form). <em>Prefer weaker.</em></td></tr>55      <tr><td class="k">Locality</td><td>How close are the two parties? Strong connascence <em>inside</em> one small module is fine; the <em>same</em> strength across a module boundary is a problem. <em>Distance raises the cost of strength.</em></td></tr>56      <tr><td class="k">Degree</td><td>How many entities are entangled? A protocol shared by 2 sites is cheaper to change than one shared by 40. <em>Prefer lower degree.</em></td></tr>57    </tbody>58  </table>5960  <div class="rulebox">61    <b>Weirich's rules of thumb</b> (how to <em>use</em> all of the above):<sup class="cite"><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>62    <ol>63      <li><b>Minimise overall connascence</b> by splitting the system into well-encapsulated elements.</li>64      <li><b>Minimise connascence that crosses boundaries</b> (long distance × any strength = pain).</li>65      <li><b>Maximise connascence within a boundary</b> — it's cheap when local, so keep entangled things together.</li>66      <li>When stuck with coupling, <b>convert a stronger form into a weaker one</b> (Position/Meaning → Name is the everyday win).</li>67    </ol>68  </div>6970  <div class="callout key">71    <span class="label">The review one-liner this buys you</span>72    Instead of "this is coupled," say: <em>"That's connascence of <b>position</b> across a module boundary — pass a named struct to make it connascence of <b>name</b>."</em> Naming the coupling and prescribing the downgrade is the whole skill.73  </div>7475  <div class="sources">76    <p style="font-family:var(--mono);font-size:0.72rem;letter-spacing:0.1em;text-transform:uppercase;color:var(--accent)">Sources</p>77    <ol>78      <li id="s1">Meilir Page-Jones, <em>What Every Programmer Should Know About Object-Oriented Design</em> (1996) &amp; "Comparing Techniques by Means of Encapsulation and Connascence," CACM 35(9), 1992.
